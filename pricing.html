Vitals measured at your visit
PulseResting heart rate

Your heart rate at rest, counted in beats per minute. It shows how hard your heart is working to pump blood around your body. A fast resting pulse can come with stress, fever, low iron or a lot of tea and coffee, and a slow one is often normal in people who exercise regularly. Our phlebotomist measures it at your visit.

Blood pressure, top numberSystolic

The top number of your blood pressure: how hard your blood pushes on your artery walls each time your heart beats. High blood pressure usually causes no symptoms, which is why checking it matters. Over the years it strains the heart, kidneys, brain and eyes. Measured at your visit.

In Standard and Premium

Blood pressure, bottom numberDiastolic

The bottom number of your blood pressure: the pressure in your arteries while your heart rests between beats. Together with the top number it shows whether your blood pressure is in a healthy range. A raised bottom number is common in younger adults and is worth keeping an eye on. Measured at your visit.

Blood count
HaemoglobinAnaemia check

The protein inside red blood cells that carries oxygen from your lungs to the rest of your body. Low haemoglobin is called anaemia and can leave you tired, weak, breathless or dizzy. It is very common in Pakistan, especially in women, and is often caused by low iron. This test shows whether your blood is carrying enough oxygen.

In Standard and Premium

RBC countRed blood cells

Counts the red blood cells that carry oxygen around your body. Read together with haemoglobin, it helps show whether you have anaemia and what kind it might be. Too few can leave you tired and pale; too many can come with dehydration or smoking.

In Standard and Premium

WBC countInfection and immunity

Counts your white blood cells, the body's defence against infection. The number often rises while you are fighting an infection or inflammation, and can fall after some viral illnesses. It gives a quick picture of how your immune system is doing.

In Standard and Premium

Platelet countClotting cells

Counts platelets, the tiny cells that clump together to stop bleeding when you get a cut. Too few can mean easy bruising or bleeding, which is why platelets are watched closely in dengue. Too many can happen with inflammation or low iron.

Blood sugar
Random blood sugarSugar at any time of day

Measures the sugar (glucose) in your blood at any time of day, with no fasting needed. It is a quick check for high blood sugar, which can be a sign of diabetes. Because you may have eaten recently, it is read against a wider range than a fasting test. A high reading is usually confirmed with HbA1c or a fasting test.

In Standard and Premium

HbA1cYour 3-month sugar average

Shows your average blood sugar over the last 2 to 3 months, not just today. It is the main test for diabetes and pre-diabetes, and for seeing how well diabetes is controlled. No fasting needed. Diabetes is very common in Pakistan and often has no symptoms in its early years.

Cholesterol the full profile
Total cholesterolAll the cholesterol in your blood

Measures all the cholesterol in your blood. Your body needs a little of this fatty substance, but too much can slowly build up in your artery walls and raise the risk of heart attack and stroke. Diet, activity, weight and family history all affect it. No fasting needed.

In Standard and Premium

LDL cholesterolThe "bad" cholesterol

Often called 'bad' cholesterol. LDL carries cholesterol into your artery walls, where it can build up as plaque and narrow them over time. A high LDL raises the risk of heart attack and stroke, which is why it is the cholesterol number doctors watch most. No fasting needed.

In Standard and Premium

HDL cholesterolThe "good" cholesterol

Often called 'good' cholesterol. HDL carries extra cholesterol away from your arteries and back to the liver to be cleared. A higher HDL is better and helps protect your heart. Regular exercise and not smoking help raise it.

In Standard and Premium

LDL/HDL ratioHeart-risk balance

Compares your 'bad' cholesterol with your 'good' cholesterol. It gives a clearer picture of heart risk than either number alone: the lower the ratio, the better. Below 3.5 is the goal.

In Standard and Premium

TriglyceridesFat in your blood

The most common type of fat in your blood. Your body turns extra calories, especially from sugar, white flour, sweets and fried food, into triglycerides. High levels add to the risk of heart disease, and very high levels can inflame the pancreas. No fasting needed for this test.

Liver and infection
SGPT (ALT)Liver health

A liver enzyme. When liver cells are under strain, ALT leaks into the blood and the level rises. Common reasons include fatty liver (linked to weight, sugar and fried food), viral hepatitis, alcohol and some medicines. A raised level is a signal to look after your liver, not a diagnosis on its own.

In Standard and Premium

Anti-HCVHepatitis C screening

Checks for hepatitis C, a virus that infects the liver and spreads through blood, for example from unsafe injections or reused needles. Many people carry it for years with no symptoms, and Pakistan has one of the highest rates in the world. Hepatitis C can now be cured. A reactive result needs a confirmation test.

HBsAgHepatitis B screening

Checks for hepatitis B, a virus that infects the liver. It spreads through blood and from mother to baby, and many people do not know they have it. A vaccine protects against it, and treatment can keep it under control. A reactive result needs follow-up with a doctor.

Kidney
CreatinineKidney health

A waste product your muscles make every day, which healthy kidneys filter out of the blood. When the kidneys are not filtering well, creatinine builds up, so it is one of the main checks of kidney health. Muscle size, how much water you drink and a high-protein meal can move it a little.

In Standard and Premium
Bones and vitamins
CalciumBones, nerves and muscles

The mineral that keeps your bones and teeth strong and helps your nerves, muscles and heart work properly. Low calcium often goes with low vitamin D, which is common in Pakistan. Levels that are too high or too low can both cause problems, so this test checks you are in the healthy middle.

In Standard and Premium

Vitamin DBone and muscle strength

Helps your body absorb calcium and keeps your bones and muscles strong. Low vitamin D is very common in Pakistan, even with plenty of sunshine, because most of us spend the day indoors or covered up. It can cause tiredness, aches and weak bones, and it is easy to put right.

Premium only
Thyroid
TSHThyroid screen

The main thyroid test. TSH is the signal your brain sends to tell the thyroid gland how much hormone to make. A high TSH usually means an underactive thyroid (tiredness, weight gain, feeling cold); a low one can mean an overactive thyroid (weight loss, a racing heart, feeling anxious). Thyroid problems are common, especially in women.

Premium only
Iron and inflammation
FerritinIron stores

Shows how much iron your body has stored. It is the best single test for iron deficiency, which is very common, especially in women and children. Low iron stores can make you tired and breathless well before your haemoglobin drops. Ferritin can also rise with inflammation.

Premium only

ESRInflammation marker

Measures how quickly red blood cells settle to the bottom of a tube. It goes up when there is inflammation somewhere in the body, for example from an infection or arthritis. It does not say where the inflammation is, so it is read alongside your other results.

No. Every test in both packages is non-fasting, so you can eat and drink as usual before your visit.

What if I can't make my visit?

Full details are in our refund and cancellation policy.

Is my health data private from my employer?

Always. If your company runs a health programme, HR sees only whether you booked and completed your check-up, never your results.
